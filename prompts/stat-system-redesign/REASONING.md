# Reasoning: Stat System Redesign — Merit

## Goal

Replace the sixth stat, Mythic Significance, with something grounded in Buddhist canonical sources. The problem was simple: Mythic Significance was too vague and subjective — there was no textual basis for assigning a number to it, making it feel invented. The replacement needed to be universally applicable to all 40 figures (monks, kings, deities, laypeople alike) and educationally meaningful.

## Iteration history

**v1 — Mythic Significance:** The initial sixth stat. Assigned based on how "cosmologically important" or "symbolically prominent" a figure was in Buddhist tradition. Problem: this cannot be verified from any text. Two people with equal knowledge of Buddhism could assign wildly different Mythic Significance scores to the same figure. Historical accuracy was estimated at ~50–60% for this stat.

**v2 — Merit (current):** Replaced Mythic Significance after asking "How accurate are these stats?" and identifying the sixth stat as the weakest. Options considered:

- **Compassion (mettā/karuṇā):** Rejected — only clearly applicable to spiritual practitioners; hard to assign to kings, nagas, or Mara.
- **Renunciation (nekkhamma):** Rejected — fits monastics better than lay supporters or cosmic beings.
- **Dhamma Knowledge:** Rejected — overlaps too heavily with the existing Wisdom stat.
- **Merit (puñña):** Chosen — applies universally to all 40 figures, and the Four Stages of Enlightenment provide a precise canonical scale.

The Etadagga Sutta (AN 1.188–234), which explicitly names the "foremost disciples" in each quality, was used as the primary source for assigning attainment levels.

## Failure modes the final version handles

- **Non-universality:** Compassion and Renunciation don't apply cleanly to Mara, kings, or deities. Merit does — Mara's low Merit (25) is theologically coherent because he embodies delusion; Anathapindika's Merit (80) reflects stream-entry through generous dana.
- **Unverifiability:** Every Merit assignment can now be defended from Pali Canon sources. Angulimala's Merit 98 = arahant level; King Bimbisara's Merit 72 = stream-enterer (explicitly stated in the suttas).
- **Stat overlap:** Merit is distinct from Wisdom (understanding) and Resolve (discipline) — it measures accumulated virtue and attainment, not cognitive or volitional qualities.

## Outcome

The Merit redesign increased estimated historical accuracy for the sixth stat from ~50–60% to ~85–90%. Players now learn something real about Buddhist attainment levels as they play — every card's Merit score tells you approximately where that figure sits on the path to enlightenment. The change also made the stat system more educational as a whole: a player can look at Angulimala (Merit 98) and understand that despite his violent past, he achieved full arahantship.

## What I'd change next

Some Merit scores for figures without explicit canonical attainment data (e.g. King Suddhodana, Queen Maya) are estimated based on inference. A footnote system in the card data noting "canonical basis" vs "estimated" would make the educational claims more transparent and defensible.
